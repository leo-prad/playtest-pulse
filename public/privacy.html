1. Introduction and defined terms

This Privacy Policy (“Policy”) explains how Playtest Pulse (“Playtest Pulse,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) handles information in connection with our playtest telemetry and feedback analytics service, comprising our hosted dashboard, our HTTP API, and our Roblox Luau software development kit (together, the “Service”).

In this Policy:

  • “Developer” means the Roblox creator, studio, or other person who registers an account with us and integrates the SDK into their Roblox experience.
  • “Player” means an end user who plays a Roblox experience that a Developer has instrumented with the SDK.
  • “Player Telemetry” means the data described in Section 4, which a Developer transmits to us from their experience.
  • “you” means whichever of the above applies to you in context.

Playtest Pulse is an independent service. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Roblox Corporation.

2. Our two roles: controller and processor

Our role under data protection law differs depending on whose data is involved. This distinction determines who is accountable for what.

  • Developer account data (Section 3) — we are the controller. We decide what account information to collect and why, and we are directly accountable to Developers for it.
  • Player Telemetry (Section 4) — we are a processor; the Developer is the controller. The Developer alone decides which events to record, what properties to attach, whether to offer a free-text feedback prompt, what notice to give Players, and what legal basis applies. We process Player Telemetry only to provide the Service to that Developer, on their instructions. We do not determine the purposes of that processing and we do not use Player Telemetry for our own purposes.

Consequently, a Player who wishes to exercise rights over Player Telemetry should contact the Developer of the relevant experience. Section 9 explains how those requests are fulfilled. Developers are responsible for satisfying their own controller obligations, including providing a privacy notice to their Players and obtaining any legally required consent.

3. Developer account data (we are the controller)

When a Developer registers or uses an account, we store:

  • Email address. Used to identify the account and to sign in.
  • Password verifier. For email-and-password accounts, we store a bcrypt hash of the chosen password (work factor 10). We do not store the password itself and cannot recover it.
  • Federated sign-in identifiers. If a Developer signs in with Google or GitHub, we store the provider name, the provider’s account identifier for that Developer, the display name the provider returns, and the time the provider was linked. We request only the profile and email scopes needed to create an account.
  • Account timestamps. Account creation time, and the time this Policy was accepted.
  • Project records. For each game a Developer creates: the name they give it, its creation time, and its API key.

Legal bases (UK/EU GDPR). We process Developer account data to perform our contract with the Developer (Article 6(1)(b)) and, for security, abuse prevention, and service integrity, on the basis of our legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)).

4. Player Telemetry (we are a processor)

The SDK runs on the Developer’s Roblox servers and transmits the following to our API. What is actually sent is determined by the Developer’s own instrumentation.

4.1 Pseudonymous player reference

We do not store a Player’s Roblox user ID, username, or display name. Instead, the SDK sends the Roblox user ID to our API, and our server immediately derives a pseudonymous player reference before the record is written: a SHA-256 digest of the game’s internal identifier concatenated with the Roblox user ID, truncated to sixteen hexadecimal characters (64 bits). The raw Roblox user ID is not persisted.

We describe this reference as pseudonymous, not anonymous, and the distinction is deliberate. The derivation is deterministic: the same Player in the same game always produces the same reference. That is what allows the Service to group a Player’s sessions together and to honour deletion requests. It also means the reference is capable of being linked back to an individual by someone who holds both the game identifier and the Player’s Roblox user ID — for example, by computing candidate digests. The derivation is namespaced per game rather than keyed with a secret value, so it should not be relied upon as a one-way anonymisation measure. Accordingly, we treat the player reference, and all records linked to it, as personal data under the GDPR and as personal information under applicable US state privacy laws, and we apply this Policy to it in full.

Because the derivation is namespaced per game, the same Player produces different references in different games, which prevents us from correlating a Player across unrelated Developers’ experiences.

4.2 Gameplay events

Each event carries a name chosen by the Developer (truncated to 120 characters), an optional free-form properties object, and two timestamps: the time the Developer’s game server reported the event occurred, and the time our server received it. We retain both because we do not treat a client-supplied clock as authoritative.

The properties object is arbitrary and entirely Developer-defined. We do not restrict, inspect, or validate its contents, and its contents therefore depend wholly on the Developer’s instrumentation. It is the Developer’s responsibility not to place personal information, special category data (GDPR Article 9), or precise location data into event properties. See Section 6.

4.3 Free-text Player feedback

If the Developer implements an in-experience feedback prompt, the text a Player submits is stored verbatim, truncated to 2,000 characters per submission, together with the submitting Player’s pseudonymous reference and timestamps.

This is a free-text field and we cannot control what a Player types into it. A Player may enter their real name, contact details, or other personal information, whether or not the Developer invited it. Developers who enable a feedback prompt are responsible for the notice, moderation, and lawful basis for that collection, and should discourage the submission of personal information in the prompt itself. This is particularly important where the audience may include children; see Section 7.

4.4 Session context

For each session we store a session identifier, the Roblox server instance identifier (JobId) reported by the SDK, or the literal value studio where the session originated in Roblox Studio; an optional region label supplied by the Developer (for example, “US East”) which is a Developer-provided label and not a geolocation we derive; and session start and end times.

4.5 Information we do not collect

  • Roblox user IDs (see Section 4.1), usernames, or display names.
  • Roblox chat, voice, direct messages, friend lists, or social graph.
  • Payment, purchase, or Robux transaction data.
  • Passwords or credentials belonging to Players.
  • Advertising, cross-site tracking, or device fingerprinting identifiers.
  • Data from any experience other than the one that transmitted it.

5. Network and operational data

Our API applies rate limiting to protect the Service from abuse. This involves processing the source IP address of incoming requests transiently in server memory for the duration of the applicable rate limit window. IP addresses are not written to our datastore and are not associated with Player Telemetry records. Our hosting provider may additionally maintain its own transport and access logs; see Section 8.

By integrating the SDK, the Developer agrees that they are responsible for:

  • Lawful basis and consent. Establishing a valid legal basis for the collection, and obtaining any consent required in the relevant jurisdiction, including verifiable parental consent where that is required for a child.

  • Compliance with Roblox rules. Ensuring that their use of the SDK, of HttpService, and of any in-experience feedback prompt complies with the Roblox Terms of Use and applicable Roblox policies, including restrictions on soliciting personal information from users and rules concerning off-platform data collection.   • Responding to Players. Receiving Player requests and using the mechanisms in Section 9 to fulfil them, or forwarding them to us.
  • API key custody. Keeping their per-game API key confidential and rotating it if it is exposed. A key permits both writing telemetry and deleting Player data for that one game.

7. Children’s privacy

The Roblox platform has a substantial number of users under the age of 13, and we treat this as a serious risk area.

The Service is offered to Developers, not to Players, and our dashboard is not directed to children. A Developer must be old enough to form a binding contract in their jurisdiction to register an account.

We do not knowingly solicit or collect personal information directly from children. We do not ask Players for their age, and by design we do not receive a Player’s Roblox user ID in retained form, name, or contact details. However, because a Developer controls what is transmitted — and because free-text feedback (Section 4.3) can contain anything a Player types — a Developer whose experience is directed to or likely to be used by children must:

  • Comply with the US Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (“COPPA”) and its rule, including any applicable verifiable parental consent requirement, and with GDPR Article 8 and equivalent local rules on children’s consent;
  • Not enable a free-text feedback prompt for a child audience unless they have a lawful basis to collect that text and are able to moderate it;
  • Not transmit any personal information about a child to the Service.

If we become aware that we hold personal information that a Developer collected from a child without a required consent, we will delete it and may suspend the Developer’s account. A parent, guardian, or Developer may request such deletion using the contact details in Section 13, and we will act without undue delay.

8. Service providers and disclosures

We do not sell personal information. We do not share personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising. We do not use Player Telemetry to train machine learning models of our own. We disclose information only as follows.

  • Hosting. The Service runs on infrastructure operated by our hosting provider, Render Services, Inc., which stores our application data and may process it in the United States. Our hosting provider also generates operational and access logs in the ordinary course of providing the platform.
  • Feedback theme extraction (conditional). The dashboard offers an optional feature that groups Player feedback into themes. When this feature is enabled on our deployment, invoking it transmits up to the 100 most recent feedback entries for the selected game — as verbatim Player-written text — to Anthropic PBC’s API for processing, and Anthropic processes it as our sub-processor in order to return the result. Where the feature is not enabled, the same analysis is performed locally on our own server by keyword frequency and no feedback text leaves our infrastructure. Developers who consider their feedback text sensitive should not enable a free-text prompt.
  • Federated sign-in providers. If a Developer chooses to sign in with Google or GitHub, that provider processes the sign-in under its own privacy policy. This affects Developers only, never Players.
  • Legal and protective disclosures. We may disclose information where we are required to do so by law, or where necessary to investigate or prevent fraud, abuse, or threats to the security or integrity of the Service or to the rights or safety of any person.
  • Business transfers. If we are involved in a merger, acquisition, financing, or sale of assets, information may be transferred as part of that transaction. We will give notice before personal data becomes subject to a materially different privacy policy.

International transfers. Our infrastructure and service providers are located in the United States. If you are in the United Kingdom, the European Economic Area, or Switzerland, transfers of your personal data to us or our sub-processors are made on the basis of appropriate safeguards, which for our sub-processors means the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses or an equivalent mechanism. You may request further information using the contact details in Section 13.

9. Data retention and deletion

We do not currently apply an automatic time-based expiry to Player Telemetry. Telemetry remains associated with a Developer’s game for as long as that game exists on their account. Deletion happens when it is triggered, as follows.

  • Deleting a game. When a Developer deletes a game from their dashboard, that game and all of its sessions, events, and feedback are deleted from our active datastore in the same operation.
  • Deleting a Developer account. Deleting an account deletes every game it owns and all associated sessions, events, and feedback in the same operation.
  • Erasing one Player. A Developer may permanently erase a single Player’s data for one of their games by calling DELETE /player/:player_ref, authenticated with that game’s API key. This removes that Player’s sessions, every event belonging to those sessions, and every feedback entry linked to their pseudonymous reference, and returns a count of the records removed. The operation is scoped to the single game the API key belongs to.

Deletion is immediate in our active datastore. Residual copies may persist for a limited period in encrypted backups or in our hosting provider’s logs before being overwritten in the ordinary course. We may retain the minimum information necessary to comply with a legal obligation or to resolve a dispute.

Demonstration sandboxes created by the “Try the demo” feature contain synthetic sample data only, contain no real Player data, and are not currently purged on a fixed schedule.

10. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have some or all of the following rights in relation to your personal data: to be informed; to access a copy; to have inaccurate data corrected; to have data erased; to restrict or object to processing; to data portability; to withdraw consent where processing is based on consent; and not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing. The Service does not carry out automated decision-making or profiling that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

If you are in California, you may also have the right to know what personal information we collect and how it is used and disclosed, to request deletion or correction, and to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information. As stated in Section 8, we do not sell or share personal information. We will not discriminate against you for exercising any right.

How to exercise a right. If you are a Developer, contact us using Section 13. If you are a Player, please contact the Developer of the experience you played, because they are the controller and, as explained in Section 4.1, we hold only a pseudonymous reference and cannot on our own identify which records relate to you. If you contact us directly, we will refer your request to the relevant Developer where we can identify them, and will assist them in fulfilling it.

If you are in the UK or EEA and consider that we have not handled your personal data properly, you have the right to lodge a complaint with your local supervisory authority. In the UK this is the Information Commissioner’s Office.

11. Security

We maintain technical and organisational measures appropriate to the Service, including:

  • Storing Developer passwords only as bcrypt hashes, never in plaintext or reversible form;
  • Authenticating dashboard access with signed, expiring session tokens that renew on activity and lapse after three days of inactivity;
  • Separating trust boundaries, so that a per-game API key can write telemetry and erase Player data for that one game but cannot read any dashboard, and a dashboard session cannot be used as an API key;
  • Allowing Developers to rotate a per-game API key at any time;
  • Rate limiting and request size limits on public endpoints;
  • Transport encryption (HTTPS) for traffic to our hosted deployment;
  • Deriving the pseudonymous player reference server-side before any record is written, so the raw Roblox user ID is never persisted.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. Developers materially affect the security of their own Player Telemetry through their handling of their API key and their choice of what to transmit.
